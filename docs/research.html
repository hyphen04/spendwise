Executive summary

India's money reality in 2026 is defined by UPI fragmenting spending into hundreds of tiny transactions, a salary-cycle crunch where 60%+ of urban salaried households have under a month saved, forgotten UPI Autopay subscriptions (1.27B mandates, 10× in <2 years), and deep distrust of apps that want bank/SMS access or sell data. Meanwhile the personal-finance app category is in a retention crisis: ~80% abandon a budgeting app within a month, the average person tries 3 before giving up, and standalone subscription finance apps keep dying (Mint, Clarity Money, Piggy, Muvin, Investmint, Plus Gold, Dream Money) — sometimes permanently deleting user data.

The evidence supports the user's thesis: subscription SaaS for personal finance is structurally fragile, and consumers are fatigued. A one-time or free, local-first, export-friendly tool that doesn't sell data or force cloud signup aligns with both the global subscription-fatigue data and India-specific privacy reluctance. The differentiator is trust, ownership, and not being abandoned — not "AI chat" (which is demonstrably unreliable for finance: a UGA study found a chatbot told one demographic to keep no cash).

1. Competitor app reviews (Indian audience)

Dying-app distrust (the canonical stories)

Cheetah Mobile — ~45 apps (4.5B+ installs) removed from Play Store Feb 2020 for ad fraud & "disruptive ads" (ads during calls/navigation). The reference "dying app / can't trust the developer" story. Android Police · The Verge · BuzzFeed · Android Authority.

Dream Money (Dream Sports wealthtech) — shut July 30, 2026, <1 year after launch; SIPs cancelled July 7; holdings migrated. A live, India-specific dying-startup saga. ET · Entrackr · Angel One.

Walnut (now Axio)

Loved: auto-reads bank SMS to categorize expenses without account linking; was India's most-downloaded tracker. TrackMyRupee · Medium.

The big complaint: the privacy/lending pivot. Acquired by Capital Float (2018), rebranded to Axio, became a BNPL/lending platform — "We are sacrificing financial privacy for a little bit of convenience." Categorization "not always accurate"; bank-sync issues; manual-entry requests ignored. TrackMyRupee · MouthShut · Substack

Money Manager Expense & Budget (Realbyte)

4.6/5, ~4.56L reviews, 1Cr+ downloads in India. Play Store. Loved for no bank-linking, double-entry, Excel backup, low one-time cost. Recurring complaints:

  • Recurring transactions bug — repeated bills don't show on the monthly calendar; must be re-entered manually.
  • No cross-device sync — the single biggest complaint; "in progress" for years.
  • No split transactions; can't delete a single digit (whole number deletes); clipboard privacy scare; no onboarding; dated UI; intrusive ads; Indian date-format friction. JustUseApp

Monefy

Loved: lightweight, one-tap entry, simple circular chart. Complaints:

  • One-time → subscription bait-and-switch — long-time users lost access to previously paid features (e.g. dark theme) with no refund.
  • Sync unreliable (Dropbox/Drive data loss); no bank sync; no recurring automation; poor support (emails bounce). Chrome-stats · Omellody

Spendee, Goodbudget, YNAB-in-India

  • Spendee — subscription required for anything useful; free version "very limited"; bank-sync discrepancies; privacy concerns. SaaSHub · SidePaisa
  • Goodbudget — envelope budgeting; manual entry in free; premium for more envelopes/multi-device; limited reporting. Retirewise
  • YNAB — no Indian bank sync (Plaid covers few/no Indian institutions); ~₹9,000/yr for fully manual entry; "a free spreadsheet can replicate the method." No direct r/IndiaPersonalFinance threads found — flagged. FinCompareLab · What Reddit Thinks

KhataBook / Bahi Khata

4.4★, ~5.87L reviews, 5Cr+ downloads. Loved: offline-first auto-sync, QR payments, 10+ regional languages — "you can continue using if your internet connection is lost."

Serious complaints: payment settlement failures (₹3,500–₹50,000 stuck/not credited for days; "cyber crime" complaints filed); unresponsive support; merchants want WhatsApp reminders instead of SMS ("people often ignore SMS"). Consumer Complaints Court · Trustpilot · Techjockey · AppFollow

Paytm / PhonePe in-app spend views

Paytm "Spend Summary" & PhonePe "Expense Tracker" auto-categorize UPI — but each only sees its own app's transactions. Most Indians use 2–4 UPI apps, so each tracker is a fragmented slice; the bank statement (CSV) is the only comprehensive truth. Paytm · Moonproduct · Learnfinedge

Jupiter / Fi / INDmoney

  • Fi — strongest insights ("Ask Fi", Insights Hub; users found ₹15,000 in forgotten subscriptions, ₹8,500/mo food delivery). Customer support is the #1 weakness; tiered pricing added fees (₹236/yr debit card). Aayush Bhaskar · Mani Karthik
  • Jupiter — basic categorization, gamified "Jewels," instant loans. App Store
  • INDmoney — comprehensive (Indian+US stocks, MFs, FDs, EPF, NPS); free tier funded by referral arrangements; recommendations algorithmic, not fiduciary. InvestKaro · Foliyo

Splitwise (the "who owes whom" incumbent)

Aggressive paywalling since 2023 — free users limited to ~3 expenses/day, 10-second full-screen ads before adding expenses, search & receipt-scan locked behind Pro (~₹330–400/mo, "too expensive for a bill-splitting utility"). No native UPI — users manually copy account details and switch apps. India-specific alternative FairShare offers unlimited free + native UPI deep-linking. TechnoFino · FairShare · ComplaintsBoard

2. Indian spending & saving reality

UPI dominance & "too many tiny transactions"

  • 228.3–228.5B UPI transactions in CY2025 (₹300 lakh crore), +33% YoY. Hindu BL
  • Avg ticket falling: >₹1,600 (early 2023) → ~₹1,293 (Dec 2025); merchant (P2M) avg ₹592; groceries ₹214, fast food ₹113, restaurants ₹158. SBI Research · FE
  • 504M unique UPI users (Aug 2025); UPI = 84% of retail digital payments (FY25). Hindu BL
  • Implication: hundreds of sub-₹500 transactions/month make manual entry unmanageable; auto-categorization essential, but any single-app tracker is incomplete because users span 2–4 UPI apps. Moonproduct

Salary-cycle cashflow / month-end crunch

  • RBI Household Finance Survey 2024: 60%+ of urban salaried households have <1 month of expenses saved. Rivo.pe
  • Lifestyle inflation: 70–80% of a salary increment consumed within 6 months.
  • EMI normalization: 3–5 simultaneous EMIs, ₹15,000–35,000/mo; when EMIs exceed 30–35% of take-home, the system is fragile. Business Today
  • The viral "₹43,000 → ₹7 in 5 minutes" post: rent ₹19k, CC minimum ₹15k (of ₹60k), two EMIs ₹10k. RBI: personal loans +75% in 3 yrs; household debt 41.9% of GDP; national household savings at a 47-year low.
  • The estimation gap: people underestimate spending by 20–30% — food delivery thought ₹1,000, actually ₹2,800. Paisewaise · ET

Festival / seasonal spending

  • Diwali 2024 urban spend ~₹1.85T (LocalCircles, 49,000+ responses, 342 districts). Business Standard
  • YouGov Diwali Spending Index 2024: 117.37 (4-yr high); 34% planned to spend more. YouGov
  • Diwali 2025 sales: record ₹6.05T (+25% YoY). Business Standard
  • Avg urban professional spends ₹80,000–₹1,50,000/yr on weddings/gifting/group dinners — unbudgeted. Rivo.pe

Family / joint finances

  • Money flows beyond the married couple — parents↔adult children, siblings, extended family. Singh 2012, Sociological Review
  • NFHS-5 (51,758 women): only 3.38% decide independently; 81.74% jointly; 14.88% dependently. Working women 1.52× more likely to be independent. PLoS ONE 2025
  • Implication: a single-user app leaves partner-side spending invisible — a noted failure mode. Moonproduct

Cash-in-hand still common

  • Cash ≈ 50% of Private Final Consumption Expenditure (Q1 FY26, Care Edge); currency in circulation ₹41.6T, +11.9% YoY. Hindu BL
  • ~86% of P2M UPI & ~60% of P2P UPI below ₹500 — UPI handles small daily; cash serves informal/large/precautionary uses. SBI Ecowrap
  • Implication: cash transactions are invisible to SMS auto-trackers — manual entry needed. Moonproduct

Lack of formal budgeting culture / low financial literacy

  • 80%+ of rural households don't maintain monthly budgets. Billcut
  • Finnovate Survey (July 2024): avg financial fitness score 5.29/20. 40% lack an emergency fund; only 38% debt-free; 73% uninsured/underinsured for health; 65% short for retirement. Business Standard
  • 71% of young Indians consider themselves financially literate, but 62% use YouTube as their primary source. BNTW

Credit-card growth + misuse

  • Credit-card NPAs +28.42% to ₹6,742 crore (Dec 2024) — a 500%+ jump since 2020. IE
  • Swipe-spend-default cycle: 42–48% annual interest traps users into minimum dues, then loan-stacking; 22% now hold 3+ cards (vs 12% a decade ago). ET
  • UPI + RuPay credit made credit frictionless down to ₹10 chai. Zavo: avg CC debt per user ₹4.16L; 13% pay only minimum at 53% annual interest. The Core · IE-CIBIL

Subscription fatigue / UPI Autopay

  • UPI Autopay doubled YoY: ~926M transactions (Nov 2025); 1.27B mandates (Nov 2025); 10× in <2 years. ET · RBI
  • The "₹1 trial" trap: sign-up authorization screen "looks like a regular payment confirmation, not a recurring agreement." Users forget; six forgotten subscriptions hit at once and disrupted an EMI. BS
  • Fi users found ₹15,000/month in forgotten subscriptions. Aayush Bhaskar

No emergency-fund habit

  • Global Findex 2025: main emergency-money source within 30 days — family/friends 34.5%, working 22.7%, savings only 14.3%, selling assets 10.7%, lender 13%, couldn't 3.8%. World Bank
  • 88% anticipate high financial uncertainty in the next 5 years; 36% cite job loss as top worry. Business Today

Gold / savings behavior

  • 85–99% of Indian households own gold; avg household holds 11% of wealth in gold; poorer households 24%. 80–90% of motivations are financial (security, liquidity, collateral). IIMA–Dvara

Informal lending/borrowing among friends & family

  • IHDS-II: ~26.2% of households borrowed from friends/relatives (vs 24.6% applying for bank loans). Springer
  • CMIE CPHS: for every 2 borrowing institutionally, 5 borrow non-institutionally; ratio 2.63× vs 0.6× in Brazil. Scroll · Piramal
  • The "forgetting" problem: Splitwise exists precisely because people lose track of who owes whom; its 1Cr+ India downloads confirm the need — and its paywalling confirms the gap for a free, local who-owes-whom tracker. TechnoFino · FairShare

3. Ground-level problems to solve (Top 15, ranked)

  1. UPI created hundreds of tiny monthly transactions nobody can track manually, and each UPI app only sees its own slice — 228B+ UPI txns in 2025, avg ticket ₹1,293 (merchant ₹592), most use 2–4 apps. Hindu BL · Moonproduct → Phase 1–2 (AI picks useful aggregations, not raw row dumps)
  2. People forget who owes whom among friends & family, and Splitwise paywalled basic features — 26.2% of households borrow from friends/relatives; Splitwise limited free users to ~3 expenses/day with ads; no native UPI. Springer · TechnoFino → Existing Dues feature (own it, keep it free, local)
  3. Forgotten UPI Autopay/recurring subscriptions silently drain accounts — 1.27B mandates (Nov 2025), 10× in <2 yrs; the "₹1 trial" trap; Fi users found ₹15k/mo forgotten. ET · BS → Phase 3 (Bills & subscriptions)
  4. Most urban salaried Indians live paycheck-to-paycheck with <1 month saved; "savings-last" is the core failure — RBI HFS 2024: 60%+ <1 month saved. Rivo.pe · BT → Phases 4 & 6 (goals + forecast/"days of money left")
  5. Apps that require bank/SMS access trigger privacy fear and high drop-off; users don't want bank data on a server — 68% AA drop-off before consent; Walnut/Axio reads all SMS incl. OTPs and sells credit products. Billcut · TrackMyRupee → SpendWise core (on-device, no account, no SMS read)
  6. Cash is still ~50% of consumption expenditure, invisible to any SMS/auto tracker — careEdge Q1 FY26; ₹41.6T currency in circulation. Hindu BL → Manual entry stays first-class (already core)
  7. Festival/seasonal spending is large, recurring, and unbudgeted (₹80k–₹1.5L/yr social; ₹1.85T Diwali 2024 urban) — no app handles festival-envelope planning well. BS · Rivo.pe → Phase 4 (goals as envelopes; festival target)
  8. ~80% abandon budgeting apps within a month; the average person tries 3 before giving up — driven by manual effort (26% of cancellations) and setup friction. gotaprob · RetentionCheck → Phase 5 (weekly digest) + no-shame tone
  9. Existing apps are built for English-literate urban salaried users; 80%+ of rural households don't budget, and vernacular onboarding gives 2.6× higher completion — a real underserved Bharat segment. Billcut Flagged: i18n is a future phase
  10. Credit-card + BNPL misuse is producing a debt trap, especially among Gen Z/millennials — CC NPAs +28% to ₹6,742 crore; 42–48% annual interest; UPI-RuPay made credit frictionless down to ₹10 chai. IE · ET Future: EMI/credit visibility
  11. Joint/family finances mean a single-user app leaves partner-side spending invisible — only 3.4% of women decide independently, 81.7% decide jointly. PLoS ONE Future: shared/household views
  12. Dying startups leave users stranded with deleted data and cancelled SIPs — Mint (data permanently deleted, no recovery), Piggy, Muvin, Investmint, Plus Gold, Dream Money all shut in ~2 yrs. CNET · ET → SpendWise core (export-first, local DB)
  13. Subscription/paywall fatigue is measurable and rising — households cut from 4.1→2.8 subscriptions (2024→25), 41% report fatigue, SaaS cancellations +23% YoY. Self Financial · Apprupt → SpendWise: free / one-time, never subscription
  14. "AI chat" in finance is demonstrably unreliable and users are losing real money following it — UGA study (July 2026): demographic bias & inconsistent advice across 7 chatbots (one told a demographic to keep no cash); 19% of US adults lost >$100 following chatbot advice. UGA · FT → SpendWise AI: deterministic insights + gatekeeper, not raw chat
  15. No emergency-fund habit; reliance on family/friends (34.5%) over savings (14.3%) — an app that gently builds an emergency-fund envelope and surfaces "days of money left" addresses a real gap. World Bank · BS → Phase 4 (goals) + Phase 6 (forecast)

4. Retention & habit formation

The brutal benchmarks

  • ~80% abandon a budgeting app within the first month; average person tries 3 before giving up. gotaprob
  • Budgeting-app monthly churn 7.9% (62.5% annual) — far above SaaS median (4.8%/mo). RetentionCheck
  • Fintech D30 retention collapsed to 2% in 2026 (−33% YoY), per Adjust — steepest decline of any category. eMarketer
  • Global finance-app day-1 retention fell 13.8% (2023) → 12.5% (H1 2025); banking apps are the standout at 20.6%. Adjust/BW

Why users cancel budgeting apps specifically

  1. Achieved goal / no longer needed — 29%
  2. Too much manual effort — 26%
  3. Free alternatives (bank apps, spreadsheets) — 22%
  4. Privacy concerns about linking accounts — 14%
  5. Bank-connection sync issues — 9%  RetentionCheck

What makes apps sticky

  • Low-friction entry: strong auto-sync retains at 5–7%/mo churn vs 9–12% for manual; manual-entry apps abandoned within ~3 weeks. RetentionCheck · Moonproduct
  • Reframe from "budget tracking" to "financial coaching": net-worth tracking + personalized insights retain 2–3× longer; YNAB's retention comes from methodology + community, not the software. RetentionCheck
  • Trials boost D30 retention to 42% vs 23% without. RetentionCheck
  • Engagement drives outcomes: users who increased engagement saw 1.8× greater change in deposit balances and 3.9× in loan accounts (MX, 30M+ consumers over 24 mo). MX
  • The real user need: users "want to feel less anxious about money" — a different outcome than tracking; the most effective interventions "require the least ongoing effort." gotaprob
  • Tracking ≠ behavior change: dashboards that look identical every visit make opening the app feel pointless; apps with all of {education, scoring, guidance, accountability} retain users. FFP

Habit-formation mechanics

  • Hook Model (trigger → action → variable reward → investment) & cue→routine→reward; heavily applied in fintech. Medium
  • Duolingo's 7-day-streak users are 2.4× more likely to continue; SaverLife "Race to $500" produced a 31% increase in savings rates. earmarkIQ
  • Behavioral economics: present bias (auto-deposits/round-up), loss aversion (frame saving as not-loss), status-quo bias (opt-out defaults), Thaler & Benartzi "Save More Tomorrow" (commit future pay-increases to savings → quadrupled savings rates). Sue

The dying-app risk (standalone subscription fragility)

Mint (2024), Clarity Money (2021), Simple, Finn, Zeta (2025), Money Dashboard (2025), Moneyhub (2026) all shut down — pure-budgeting subscription models can't sustain themselves. RetentionCheck. In India: Piggy, Muvin, Investmint, Plus Gold, Dream Money all shut within ~2 years. ET-Piggy · Entrackr-Muvin · Entrackr-Investmint · Inc42-PlusGold · Entrackr-DreamMoney.

5. No-shame tone (design principle)

The "wellness fintech" movement is a direct reaction against red-number/guilt design. Shame corrodes the very part of us that believes we're capable of change — guilt leads to avoidance, not better behavior.

  • Money Mirror (Pratt, May 2026): no red in the UI, no scores/grades/failure-states, patterns framed as observations not alerts, ask "did it help?" not "was this in budget?" Medium
  • Track That Money (Flutter, for neurodivergent users): avoids red, uses soft temperature colors — "awareness replaces shame; the user learns to observe rather than judge." GitHub
  • Good With (CBT-based): 57% of learners feel less anxious after 2 weeks, 52% more confident. Good With
  • Affect labeling (naming emotions reduces amygdala activity) is the neurological basis for reflection-over-enforcement. Also see MoodWallet, enough.app ("judgment-free"). MoodWallet · enough.app
SpendWise application: avoid #DC2626 (red) as a "you failed" cue; frame over-budget/over-spend as observation ("Spent ₹4,200 of ₹4,000 — 5% over"), never alarm; no scores/grades/failure-states; warm/soft palette for status. A shared lib/app/utils/tone.dart copy helper ("on track" / "a bit over" / "worth a look") is applied across all new UI in Phases 3–6. The existing AiGatekeeper numeric-sanity conservatism extends to all notification + nudge copy.

6. Anti-patterns to avoid

  1. Aggressive paywalls / bait-and-switch — Monefy moved one-time→subscription and locked previously paid features with no refund; Splitwise limited free users to ~3 expenses/day with 10s ads. Chrome-stats · TechnoFino
  2. Forced account/cloud signup — 68% of AA users drop off before granting consent; the consent UX looks like "legal jargon, not empowerment." Billcut
  3. Ads — Cheetah Mobile's 45 apps banned for disruptive ads. The Verge
  4. Data-selling / privacy concerns — Walnut/Axio reads all bank SMS (incl. OTPs) and pushes credit products; SBI's MD flagged "single consent for everything"; security/privacy concerns drive 26.8% of FinTech adoption reluctance in India. TrackMyRupee · JIER
  5. Complex setup / no onboarding — Money Manager has "no tutorial" and a steep learning curve; YNAB runs dedicated workshops. JustUseApp · gotaprob
  6. Requiring bank connection — bank-connection failures cause 20–30% mid-onboarding abandonment; YNAB has near-zero Indian bank support. RetentionCheck · FinCompareLab
  7. Dying startups leaving users stranded — Mint permanently deleted all user data after Mar 23, 2024 (no recovery); Piggy cancelled SIPs; Plus Gold users had withdrawal troubles. CNET · Spendify
  8. "AI chat" that adds no real value — UGA study (July 2026): 7 chatbots gave "significantly varied" advice with demographic bias; one told African Americans to keep no cash. 19% of US adults lost >$100 following chatbot advice. The "jagged frontier": reliable on common topics, dangerously wrong on rare/expensive one-time decisions. UGA · FT · The Conversation
  9. Generic advice not tuned to India — US-centric apps have limited India bank sync (Plaid); category sprawl ("30+ categories → daily tagging miserable → app abandoned"); merchant-name confusion (UPI receipts show razorpay@hdfcbank not "Cafe Coffee Day"). Finny · Moonproduct

7. SaaS fatigue & the local-first appeal

Consumer subscription fatigue is real and measurable

  • Avg US household subscriptions dropped 4.1 → 2.8 (2024→2025), a 32% decline. Self Financial
  • 52% canceled ≥1 subscription in the past year; SaaS cancellations +23% YoY in 2025; 41% report subscription fatigue. Apprupt · MarketWatch
  • Wasted spend: ~$200/yr on unused subscriptions; consumers estimate $86/mo but actually spend $219/mo (a $133 gap). Apprupt
  • 70% locked into a paid subscription because they forgot to cancel a free trial; 76% believe services intentionally make cancellation difficult (avg 6.2 "dark patterns"). Apprupt
  • In India: the ₹199–₹299/mo forgotten-subscription problem disrupts household cashflow; Splitwise Pro at ₹330–₹400/mo is "too expensive for a bill-splitting utility." BS · TechnoFino

The appeal of local-first / own-your-data

  • Local-first movement (2019 Ink & Switch manifesto) gaining momentum on the back of cloud shutdowns — Omnivore (Nov 2024), Pocket (Mozilla shut July 2025, ~35M libraries lost). Core: "Your data. Your devices. Your rules." outl · DEV
  • Honest critique: self-hosting "moves risk from a company with an ops team to a person who has a day job." The more important properties are escapable (can you export everything?) and machine-readable — not just local-vs-cloud. DEV
  • 2026 trend: local-first apps expose data to LLMs via MCP so AI agents can read local files without data leaving the device — positioning as privacy-preserving AI. outl
  • India-specific signal: TrackMyRupee (indie, Pune) markets as "Zero data sharing, no SMS reading, no credit product upselling. Your data is your data" — recommended as the best choice for most Indian salaried professionals. TrackMyRupee
Synthesis: the evidence supports the user's thesis. Subscription SaaS for personal finance is structurally fragile (high churn, low ARPU, dying startups) and consumers are fatigued. A one-time or free, local-first, export-friendly tool that doesn't sell data or force cloud signup aligns with both the global subscription-fatigue data and India-specific privacy reluctance. The differentiator is not "AI chat" — it is trust, ownership, and not being abandoned.

8. What we'll build (problem → phase mapping)

Phase 0
This report (HTML + RESEARCH.md).
Phase 1
Chart-spec DSL + named on-device data providers + spec→fl_chart renderer. Makes the report spec-driven with a safe default. Reuses existing repository aggregations; no LLM, no SQL yet.
Phase 2
LLM emits the chart spec (Hybrid: named providers by default + opt-in LLM-authored SQL through a read-only safety pipeline). Refined, Indian-audience-tuned system prompt. Privacy: LLM sees only schema metadata + opaque labels, never raw rows or names.
Phase 3
Bills & subscription reminders (problem #3). New recurring_items table; surfaces the existing on-device recurring detector as actionable reminders + local notifications.
Phase 4
Goals & savings targets (problems #4, #7, #15). New goals table; "Save More Tomorrow" commitment framing; festival/emergency envelopes.
Phase 5
Weekly digest + notifications (problem #8). On-device Sunday summary; optional LLM polish via the existing anonymize→LLM→restore pipeline.
Phase 6
Cashflow forecasting / run-rate nudges (problems #4, #15). "You usually spend ~₹X on Fuel by now" observation + month-end balance projection. No-shame tone.
Phase 7
.claude project tooling — skills (release, db-schema-change, privacy-audit, add-provider) + agents (explorer, privacy-auditor, reviewer) + tone.dart copy helper.

Cross-cutting: no-shame tone applied across Phases 3–6; every DB-schema phase runs the DB Schema Change Checklist; every AI change runs a privacy audit; every list screen follows the List Row Interaction Rules. Privacy invariant (no personal details leave the device) is paramount and unchanged.

Notes on source quality & gaps

  • No specific app literally named "Cheetah Money Manager" was found; the closest match is the Cheetah Mobile Play Store ban (2020) for ad fraud — the user's "Cheetah shutdown saga" likely refers to this. Flagged rather than invented.
  • No direct r/IndiaPersonalFinance threads on YNAB were surfaced in this search (results were US-Reddit). The claim that Indian YNAB users rely on manual entry is inferred from the absence of Indian bank Plaid support, not a direct Indian-user testimonial.
  • "Amazon acquired Axio in 2025" appeared in one secondary blog (Finny) but could not be verified against a primary news source — treat with caution.
  • The "80% abandon within a month" and "average person tries 3 apps" figures come from gotaprob, an analysis blog rather than a peer-reviewed study — strong directional signal, not a hard primary stat.
  • All UPI/Autopay/credit-card statistics are from NPCI/RBI/SBI Research/primary news and are recent (2024–2026).
